# Brand and Communications

Status: **in force**, 22 November 2026. Classification: **Internal**; approved
public copy is **Public**.

The brand is an asset. It is protected by the trademark rules in
[IP_POLICY.md](IP_POLICY.md), Section 8, and by the discipline in this document.
A reputation built over years can be damaged by one careless claim.

---

## 1. What the brand stands for

DUST-OS is a from-scratch x86-64 operating system with a hybrid kernel
architecture, built in-house from the boot path up.

The brand should signal:

- **craft** — the work is understood, not copied;
- **honesty** — the status is stated plainly, including what does not work;
- **discipline** — decisions, contracts, and releases are written down;
- **longevity** — the project is built to last, not to trend.

It should not signal hype, secrecy for its own sake, or borrowed credibility.

## 2. Name

| Item | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Official name | **DUST-OS** |
| Prose form | "DUST-OS" at the start of a sentence; "the system" or "the project" afterwards |
| Incorrect forms | `DustOS`, `Dust-OS`, `DUST OS`, `Dust OS` in official copy |
| Descriptor | "a from-scratch x86-64 operating system" |
| Abbreviation | Do not abbreviate to "DOS" or "Dust" in customer-facing material |

The name is a trademark of the Owner. Do not use it in a domain, product name,
company name, event name, or social handle without written permission.

## 3. Claims policy

Every public statement must be true today, not aspirational.

| Do not say | Until it is true |
| --- | --- |
| "secure" | A threat model, review, and disclosure process exist |
| "production-ready" | A supported release exists and passes the release gates |
| "open source" / "source-available" | Never — the project is private and proprietary |
| "Unix-like" / "POSIX-compatible" | The compatibility layer exists and passes tests |
| "supports hardware X" | It boots and passes the test matrix on X |
| "faster than Y" | There is a reproducible benchmark and a fair comparison |
| "AI-powered" | There is a shipped capability that justifies the phrase |
| "enterprise-grade" | There are enterprise customers, support, and evidence |

Rules:

- State the pre-alpha status whenever a reader could assume otherwise.
- Distinguish "designed to", "planned", and "works today".
- Do not imply partnerships, customers, investors, or certifications that do not
  exist.
- Do not use another company's logo or name to imply endorsement.
- Correct a false claim quickly and publicly where it was made.

## 4. Voice and tone

- **Precise.** Prefer a concrete fact to an adjective.
- **Calm.** No exclamation marks, no hype, no "revolutionary".
- **Technical.** Explain the mechanism, not just the benefit.
- **Respectful.** Do not attack other projects or people to position DUST-OS.
- **Consistent.** Use the same terms as the documentation: boot application,
  kernel, boot contract, memory map.
- **Short.** A sentence that can be cut usually should be.

Avoid: "seamless", "next-generation", "game-changing", "blazing fast",
"military-grade", "unhackable", "fully autonomous".

## 5. Visual identity

| Element | State |
| --- | --- |
| Logo | Not finalised; do not invent or publish one |
| Colour palette | Not finalised |
| Typography | Documentation uses the repository's monospace and system sans stack |
| Diagrams | Greyscale or one accent colour; label axes and units |
| Screenshots | Only from a build that exists; no mockups presented as real |

Until a logo is approved:

- use the wordmark "DUST-OS" set in a clean sans-serif;
- do not use third-party artwork, fonts, or icons without a compatible licence;
- do not put the name on merchandise or event material without the Owner's
  approval.

## 6. Public channels

| Channel | Who may speak | Approval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repository | Team members in issues and reviews | Normal review |
| Project website | Owner or appointed editor | Owner |
| Social media | Owner or appointed editor | Owner |
| Press and interviews | Owner | Owner |
| Conference talk | Named speaker | Owner |
| Customer reference | Owner and customer | Written consent from both |

Rules:

- One voice in a crisis: the Owner or a named spokesperson.
- Do not announce a release, customer, partnership, or funding before it is signed
  and the other party agrees.
- Do not post source code, internal screenshots, customer names, or architecture
  details marked Confidential.
- Do not argue with critics in public. Correct facts once, privately if possible.

## 7. Customer references and case studies

- Obtain written permission before naming a customer or showing their product.
- Let the customer review the exact text before publication.
- Do not imply that a customer uses more than they do.
- Keep the permission record with the contract.

## 8. Crisis communication

1. Stop and establish the facts.
2. Contain the technical issue; see [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md).
3. Agree on one spokesperson and one message.
4. State what is known, what is not known, and when the next update will come.
5. Do not speculate, blame, or minimise.
6. Notify affected customers privately before any public statement, unless law or
   safety requires otherwise.
7. Publish a short post-incident note when the fix is available.

## 10. Review

This document is reviewed when a logo, website, release, customer, or public
campaign is created, and at least once a year.

Questions about a public statement go to the Owner before it is published. The
cost of asking is minutes; the cost of an untrue claim is the reputation the
project is trying to build.
